AI shopping assistant

A shop assistant that never clocks off.

Ovi is the assistant on our own site. It knows the page you’re on, answers only from a knowledge sheet we write with you, and hands the chat to a person when that’s the better answer.

  • Opens with a line that fits the page — product, collection or cart
  • States only what’s in your knowledge sheet: prices, delivery, returns, sizing
  • Offers WhatsApp or a call when a shopper is stuck or frustrated
  • Saves name and email to Shopify as a lead — no marketing opt-in unless you add one
  • Thumbs on every reply, and a weekly review of real conversations

Cart drawer · discounts and gifts

Show shoppers what’s one item away.

A progress bar at the top of the cart drawer turns “free shipping over $75” into a goal. Add a second tier and a free gift appears in the cart the moment it’s earned.

  • Up to five tiers: free shipping, free gift, whatever you choose. Every tier is a reason to add one more item
  • Message updates as the cart changes — “$44 more unlocks your free gift”
  • Free gift appears under the items with an “Add for free” button
  • Tiers can run on dates, so a seasonal tier switches itself on and off
  • Works in the drawer and on the cart page; colours match your store
Best forStores with a free-shipping threshold
Typical effortMedium build

Our honest take. Set the first tier just above your average order. Too high and it feels out of reach; too low and it gives margin away. When no sale is running, a free gift can do the job a discount would, and your prices stay put.

    • Sits quietly under the items — never blocks checkout
    Best forStores with refills, accessories or small add-ons
    Typical effortQuick to medium

    Product finder · quiz

    Three questions. One right answer.

    When the range is wide, people stall. A short quiz narrows it down and ends on a product, a kit or a plan. On Halm’s store, a soil test leads to a fertiliser plan matched to each lawn.

    • Three to five questions, one per screen, mobile-first
    • Result page with the matched product and an Add to cart button
    • Answers can be saved to the customer, ready for follow-up emails
    • Your logic, not a template — the quiz knows your range
    Best forWide ranges, skin, soil, size, fit
    Typical effortMedium build

    Our honest take. Don’t ask more than you use. Every extra question loses people; we write the logic first, then the questions.

    Product page · mobile

    Say when it arrives. Keep the button close.

    Two small things that remove hesitation on a phone: a plain delivery date, worked out from your real cut-off time, and a buy bar that appears once the main button scrolls out of view.

    • “Order within 2h 14m for delivery by Thu 10 Dec” — from your cut-off and carrier times
    • Holiday cut-offs are easy to switch on for the season
    • After the last ship-by date, the same spot switches to digital gift cards
    • Sticky buy bar shows the product, price and variant, then adds to cart
    • Small, fast and light — no extra app scripts on the page
    Best forSeasonal and gifting traffic on mobile
    Typical effortQuick build

    Our honest take. The date must be true. If your carrier times aren’t reliable, we’ll suggest a softer line like “ships within 2 working days” instead.

    First visit · email capture

    A welcome offer that behaves itself.

    A single, calm offer for new visitors, timed after they’ve had a look around. Enter an email and the discount applies itself at checkout, with no code to copy and lose.

    • Appears after a delay or scroll — never the instant the page loads
    • Auto-applied discount, so nobody abandons over a missing code
    • Email goes straight into your email tool, with consent handled properly
    • Won’t show to returning customers or to anyone who closed it
    Best forStores growing an email list
    Typical effortQuick to medium

    Our honest take. A discount trains people to wait for one. We’d usually pair it with a clear reason — first order, or a starter kit — rather than an always-on pop-up.

    Reviews work hardest where the decision happens. A slider and summary near the buy button, with photo reviews and filters, instead of a block buried at the bottom of the page. Halm’s store holds a 4.8 star average from 400+ customer reviews.

    • Star summary under the product title, linked to the full list
    • Review slider on the home page and product pages
    • Photo reviews and filters by rating or topic
    • Works with the review app you already use
    Best forAny store with real reviews
    Typical effortQuick build

    Our honest take. Real, mixed reviews convert better than perfect ones, and we never invent them. If you have few reviews, start with collecting more.
