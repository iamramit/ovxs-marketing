# OVXS voice

Every piece of OVXS content follows this file. When Ramit corrects a draft and the correction applies beyond that one piece, add it under "Learned rules" at the bottom.

## Who we are

Studio OVXS is a small, senior, Shopify-first studio with more than ten years on Shopify. We design, build and look after Shopify stores, move businesses to Shopify, and stay on as a long-term partner. Senior people do the work, not a sales team.

## Who we write for

- **Founders opening their first store.** They're unsure what they need and worried about wasting money.
- **Brands moving to Shopify** from WooCommerce, Squarespace, Wix and others. They're scared of losing data, rankings and sales.
- **Growing Shopify stores** whose store looks fine but doesn't sell enough. They want someone honest to tell them why.

Default audience when a brief says "Everyone": a founder or marketing lead at a small or mid-size store who is busy, a bit sceptical of agencies, and wants a straight answer.

## How we sound

- Honest, plain, reassuring. A small senior team talking straight.
- We → you. "We'll tell you what's worth doing, even when the honest answer is less work for us."
- Say the uncomfortable thing. If an app or a quick fix will do the job, say so.
- Short declarative sentences. One idea per line on social.
- No hype, no self-help tone, no exclamation marks, no emoji in copy.
- Sentence case for headings, titles and slide text.
- Numbers stated plainly: "$99", "from $3,000", "3–6 weeks", "10+ years".
- Spaced em dashes ( — ), curly quotes and apostrophes, middots in eyebrows ("Video audit · $99").
- British-leaning spelling, as on the site: colour, prioritise, fulfilment.

## Words

Use: honest, plain, straight, senior, fixed price, walkthrough, store, shoppers, sales.

Avoid: solutions, synergy, leverage, unlock, elevate, game-changer, cutting-edge, seamless, robust, "in today's fast-paced world", "let's dive in", rhetorical "Imagine…" openers.

## Headline shape

A short declarative line, then an italic payoff. In designs the payoff is wrapped in `<em>` and gets the wavy underline.

- "Pretty store. *Poor sales.*"
- "Business first. *Design second.*"
- "Four steps. *No surprises.*"

## Hard rules

- Location: OVXS works remotely, and times are US Eastern. Never name or hint at any other country, city or time zone.
- No mention of AI writing the content, and no references to other studios' sites as inspiration.
- Every statistic needs a source link in the brief. If a number can't be sourced, cut it or turn it into opinion ("in our experience").
- Posting is done by Ramit, or by Claude in Chrome with Ramit approving the final Post click.

## Channel rules

**LinkedIn post (company page or Ramit)**
- The first line is the hook and must work before "…see more" (about 140 characters).
- Short lines with white space between them. → arrows for lists.
- 120–250 words. End with the CTA, then "Link in comments." Put the URL in the first comment, not the post.
- 3 hashtags at most, at the end.
- Graphic: 4:5 (1080×1350) with a photo, the headline and the CTA button.

**Facebook post**
- Shorter and warmer than LinkedIn, 60–120 words. The link can go in the post.
- One question to the reader is fine. No hashtag blocks.

**Instagram carousel**
- 8 slides, 1080×1350.
- 1: cover hook. 2: setup or spoiler. 3–7: one point per slide. 8: payoff + CTA.
- 12 words or fewer per slide headline. Photos on the cover and problem slides.
- Caption: 80–150 words. Hook line, three short lines, CTA "link in bio", then 3–5 hashtags.

**Newsletter block**
- Subject under 50 characters, plus preview text.
- 100–180 words. One idea and one CTA button.

**Article / blog (Shopify `/blogs/journal`)**
- 800–1,400 words. Italic dek under the title. Open with a concrete scene or client situation, not a definition.
- H2 per point. Close with "Business first" style takeaway and the audit CTA.
- Link one relevant case study or service page from the proof list.
- SEO: primary keyword in the title, H1, first 100 words and meta description (under 155 characters). Write a meta description in the brief.

**LinkedIn video**
- A 45–90 second script, split into talking-head lines plus a shot list.
- Hook in the first 3 seconds. Captions are always on.
