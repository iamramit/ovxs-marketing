Too many Shopify apps? Make every app earn its place

No magic number, no “uninstall everything”. One test for every app you pay for, and what to do with the ones that fail it.

A store owner opens the apps page for the first time in a year and scrolls. Reviews. A second reviews app, added because the first one didn’t show photos. A pop-up from a launch that ended two summers ago. A currency switcher. A size chart. Something called “Smart Bundles” that nobody on the team can explain.

None of these went in by mistake. Each one solved a real problem on the day it was installed. That’s exactly why too many Shopify apps is such a common problem: it never feels like a decision. It’s twenty small decisions, made one at a time, that nobody ever adds up.

We see it in most of the stores we look at. Here’s the test we use, and why we don’t believe in a magic number.

Why every app has a cost

Every app that touches your storefront is a small promise to load something. A script, a stylesheet, a widget, a call to someone else’s server. Shoppers pay for it in loading time, and you pay for it every month on your bill.

Shopify takes this seriously. To earn the “Built for Shopify” badge, an app must not reduce your storefront’s Lighthouse performance score by more than 10 points. Shopify measures it by comparing the score before and after the app is installed, across your home, product and collection pages.

Read that the other way round. Even the apps Shopify holds up as well built are allowed to cost you up to ten points each. Most apps don’t carry the badge at all.

A speed score isn’t a sales figure, and we won’t pretend one point equals one sale. But a slower store is never an advantage, and paying monthly for something that doesn’t earn is never good business.

There’s no magic number

Search for this topic and you’ll find lists that say “keep it under five apps” or “never more than ten”. We don’t think a number helps.

A store with twelve apps that each do a clear job can be in better shape than one with four that overlap. One informal scrape of more than 10,000 Shopify stores found roughly five detectable apps on an average storefront. That tells you what’s normal, not what’s right for you.

The better question isn’t “how many?” It’s “what is each one doing for sales?”

The test: every app has to earn its place

Set aside an hour. Open your apps page and your Shopify bill side by side.

1. List every app you pay for. Include the free ones that add something to the storefront. Check the bill too, because the two lists don’t always match.

2. Write the sale it helped make last month. Not what the app promised when you installed it. What it did. A reviews app that shows on every product page is easy to justify. A pop-up that nobody has looked at since launch is not.

3. Where two apps do one job, keep one. Overlaps hide in reviews, pop-ups, upsells and “trust badges”. Pick the one that does the job best and remove the other.

4. Cut the ones that can’t answer. If you can’t say what an app did for sales, it probably didn’t do much. Remove it, and watch for a week. If nobody notices, you have your answer.

Uninstalled isn’t always removed

Here’s the part that catches people out. Removing an app doesn’t always remove its code.

Some apps add snippets to your theme when you install them, and those snippets can keep loading after you uninstall. Store owners regularly find old app code still running months later, for apps they cancelled long ago.

If you’re comfortable with your theme files, duplicate your theme first and look there, never on the live one. If you’re not, this is a good moment to ask for help. It’s a common clean-up job and it usually doesn’t take long.

When an app is the right answer

This isn’t an argument against apps. We recommend them all the time. A good subscription app, a solid reviews app or a proper search app can earn its cost many times over, and building those yourself rarely makes sense.

Where apps go wrong is small jobs. A size chart, a badge or a simple banner doesn’t need a monthly subscription and a script on every page. A custom section from $250 is paid once, sits inside your theme and loads nothing extra.

One job per app. A custom section when the job is small. And nothing that can’t say what it earned.

Business first

The pattern is the same as most of what we see. Stores rarely need more things added. They need what’s already there to work harder.

Before you install the next app, or pay for a redesign to fix a slow store, give your current apps an hour with this test. You may find the store you need is already there, minus a few apps.
