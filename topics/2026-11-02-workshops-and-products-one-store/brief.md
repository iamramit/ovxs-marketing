### So what
- Founders ask for “a web experience” when what they need is one place to sell classes and products. Shopify does that with one checkout.
- Bookings are the hard part. Capacity, dates and deposits need an app, so that choice comes before the design.
- With under ten products and a few classes, the honest answer is a proven theme and a small budget, not a custom build.

## Angles
### A — One checkout, two kinds of thing
- Hook: Your workshops and your products don’t need two websites. They need one checkout.
- Argument: Shopify sells a seat in a class as easily as a product on a shelf; the real work is the booking rules, which one small app handles.
- Proof used: Naba: 15+ expert-led courses launched on Shopify; Shopify Help on selling services.
- Fits: Shopify know-how · New founders
- Risk: Reads like a plain how-to with no story; app names date quickly, so keep them out of the copy.

### B — Start with the calendar, not the theme
- Hook: Before you pick a theme, decide what happens when a class is full.
- Argument: Stores that sell classes and products break on operations — capacity, reschedules, reminders, no shipping address — so settle those rules first and the design follows.
- Proof used: Shopify Help on services; Community threads asking exactly this; Naba’s courses.
- Fits: Shopify know-how · New founders
- Risk: Operational detail can lose founders who want to see what the store will look like.

### C — The honest small version
- Hook: Under ten products and a few classes a month? You probably don’t need a custom build.
- Argument: For a first store this size, a proven theme plus one booking app gets you selling in weeks; spend the rest on photos and words.
- Proof used: New to Shopify plan $247/month + $500 setup (up to 25 products); Launch from $3,000, 3–6 weeks.
- Fits: Shopify know-how or Honest advice · New founders
- Risk: Must not read as a push for the monthly plan; give both options plainly.
