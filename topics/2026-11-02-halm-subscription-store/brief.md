## Research (2026-11-02)
### Our evidence
- Halm (voice.md, knowledge.js): subscription Shopify store with a soil test, a fertiliser plan matched to each lawn and seasonal boxes. Results: 4.8 star average rating; 400+ customer reviews; 3 seasonal deliveries a year on subscription. /blogs/case-studies/halm-club
- We have no retention or revenue numbers for Halm. Don't imply any.

### Audience language
- Thin this week: search turned up subscription-app vendor blogs on churn (Rivo, Recharge), not merchant threads. https://www.rivo.io/blog/subscription-churn
- Vendor framing to push against: “reduce churn with discounts and win-back flows”.

### What's already out there
- Most subscription content is about discounts, skip-a-month and churn tactics from app vendors.
- The gap: a subscription built around a real rhythm (the lawn needs feeding by season) and a store that makes that rhythm obvious before purchase.

### So what
- New subscribers are getting harder to win (acquisition down from 4.1% to 2.8%), so keeping the ones you have is the job.
- Halm's subscription follows the season, not a discount: three deliveries a year, matched to each lawn by a soil test.
- 400+ reviews at 4.8 stars come from customers who keep coming back. Quote the numbers exactly and claim nothing more.

## Angles
### A — Subscribe to the season, not the discount
- Hook: Halm’s customers don’t subscribe for a discount. They subscribe because a lawn needs feeding three times a year.
- Argument: Subscriptions that follow a real rhythm keep people; the store’s job is to make that rhythm obvious.
