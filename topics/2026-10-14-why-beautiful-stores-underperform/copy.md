# Why beautiful stores still underperform

*Design gets the compliments. Business gets the sales.*

A founder sends us their store. It's lovely — considered typography, great product shots, a homepage that feels like a magazine. Then they tell us conversion is under 1% and they can't work out why.

We hear this a lot. And the honest answer is usually: the design isn't the problem. Here's what usually is.

## 1. The market wasn't researched enough
A store can be flawless and still sell something people don't need, or don't need from you. Before you polish, ask: what problem does this solve, for whom, and who else solves it? If you can't answer in one sentence, no theme will fix that.

## 2. Too many apps
Every app promises one small improvement. Together they slow the page, clash with each other and make the store fragile. If an app or a quick fix will do the job, great — but audit what you've installed and remove what you don't measurably use. Speed is part of the experience.

## 3. A confusing checkout
Extra steps, forced accounts, surprise shipping costs, too many fields. Every one is a reason to leave. Count the clicks from cart to paid. Then cut.

## 4. Pricing that isn't competitive
Shoppers compare. If you're priced above the market, you need to show why — through quality, service, guarantees or story. If you're not doing that, price is doing the talking for you.

## 5. No upsells, no cross-sells, no social presence
Getting a visitor is expensive; getting more from each one is cheap. Bundles, "complete the set" suggestions and post-add-to-cart offers lift order value. And if a shopper searches your brand and finds no active social profile, that's a quiet red flag.

## 6. Nothing that builds trust
Reviews, clear returns, real contact details, press, photos of real people. Without them, a beautiful store just looks like a beautiful stranger. Credibility isn't a design layer — it's evidence.

## Business first, then design
Design is important. But the team you want knows more than design: they understand checkout, speed, pricing, merchandising and trust. Choose people who start with the business and let the design serve it.
