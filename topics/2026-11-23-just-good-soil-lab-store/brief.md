- The sale doesn’t end at checkout when the product is a result. Just Good Soil’s customers find orders, test kits and soil reports in one account.

## Angles
### A — One account, not three logins
- Hook: Your customer paid you. Why are they logging in somewhere else to get what they bought?
- Argument: When part of your product lives in another system, bring it into the store account.
- Proof used: Just Good Soil: one account for orders, test kits and soil reports.
- Fits: Proof · Growing stores
- Risk: Don’t claim fewer support tickets or repeat sales; neither is in the published results.

### B — The ten-minute lab
- Hook: A soil lab and a Shopify store that check in every 10 minutes.
- Argument: Shows the build as a simple chain: kit registered, lab tests, results arrive in the account.
- Proof used: Just Good Soil: lab results synced every 10 minutes.
- Fits: Proof · Growing stores
- Risk: Technical. The sync interval is not the lab’s turnaround time, so never suggest results in ten minutes.

### C — When an app won’t do
- Hook: Check the app store first. If your lab isn’t in it, keep reading.
- Argument: Use an app where one fits, and pay for a custom connection only when your product depends on another system.
- Proof used: Just Good Soil as the custom case; Intuit QuickBooks, 2024; Digital Commerce 360, 2025.
- Fits: Proof · Growing stores
- Risk: Close to “Your store doesn’t need another app”. Keep it on connections, and don’t say which apps the client tried.
