Every shopper asks six questions before they buy. A pretty store can answer none of them.

It’s a store we see often in audits. Lovely photos. A palette that feels like the brand. Steady traffic.

And sales that don’t match.

The owner usually blames the traffic. In our experience, the shopper just didn’t get their answers:

→ Where is it? The menu speaks your language, not theirs.
→ What exactly am I getting? Size, materials and delivery are missing.
→ Does this work on my phone? Tiny buttons, a pop-up over everything.
→ Can I trust you? No reviews, no returns policy in sight.
→ Is this easy? Surprise costs, a forced account, extra steps.
→ What do I do next? Three buttons, none of them clear.

Roughly 7 in 10 online carts are abandoned (Baymard Institute).

None of the six needs a redesign. Most need a clearer label, a fuller product page or one less step.

Good design gets attention.
Good UX makes buying easier.
