### So what
- Custom quotes range widely: 63% of agencies quoted between $1,000 and $15,000 (GoodFirms via Shopify, 2026). Buyers need to know what is included.
- Our rule is one fixed price once scope is agreed. New requests are quoted separately, never added silently.
- Be fair: a fixed price depends on a clear scope. When nobody knows the scope yet, find it first.

## Angles
### A — The price we agree is the price
- Hook: The scariest line on a web project is the one that arrives after you’ve said yes.
- Argument: Agree scope, set one price, and quote anything new before it starts.
- Proof used: OVXS pricing rule; published project prices.
- Fits: Behind the studio · Growing stores
- Risk: Can read as a guarantee on scope the client adds later. State that new requests are quoted separately.

### B — Cheap quotes leave things out
- Hook: Two quotes for the same store: $1,000 and $15,000. Both can be honest.
- Argument: The gap is often scope, so show buyers what to ask before comparing totals.
- Proof used: 63% of agencies quoted between $1,000 and $15,000 (GoodFirms via Shopify, 2026).
- Fits: Behind the studio · Growing stores
- Risk: The survey isn’t two quotes for one store. Present the hook as an example and keep it from sounding like an attack on cheaper studios.

### C — When a fixed price is a bad idea
- Hook: We quote fixed prices. Here’s when you shouldn’t accept one.
- Argument: If the scope is vague, a fixed price is a guess with padding. Settle what’s needed first.
- Proof used: One fixed price once scope is agreed, usually right after the $99 video audit.
- Fits: Behind the studio · Growing stores
- Risk: Argues against our own model for part of the piece. It needs a clear turn to how we settle scope.
