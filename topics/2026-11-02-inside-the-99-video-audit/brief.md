### Our evidence
- Audit terms (worker/src/knowledge.js, voice.md): 10–15 minute recorded walkthrough of the public site; what's working, what's costing them, three ranked fixes; no logins, no call; delivered within 3 working days; $99 up front; credited in full against any project or plan started within 60 days; full refund if the site isn't a fit.

- No r/shopify threads surfaced in search this week.

### What's already out there
- Fiverr gigs promise to find “hidden conversion killers”; several say outright they can't guarantee sales. https://fiverr.com/babakdev/audit-your-shopify-store-for-more-sales
- Paid CRO audits sell as long reports (one Gumroad listing at $999); free “I'll audit your store” offers in the Community.
- The gap: a short, senior walkthrough that ranks only three fixes, with the fee credited back and a refund if we're not the right fit.

### Facts
| Claim | Source | Year |
|---|---|---|
| Baymard benchmarked the checkouts of 344 top-grossing US and EU sites; the average site has 32 unique improvements to make | https://baymard.com/checkout-usability/benchmark | 2025 |
| 42% of US online shoppers have abandoned a cart because they were “just browsing / not ready to buy” — not every lost sale is fixable | https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate | 2025 |

### So what
- Store owners don't lack checklists. Baymard finds 32 possible checkout fixes on the average top site, which is why ranking three matters more than listing all of them.
- People asking for “honest feedback” mostly get vendors who can't promise anything. A senior walkthrough with a refund is a clear, honest offer.
- Some lost sales aren't fixable (42% of abandoners were just browsing), so the audit's job is to find the ones that are.

## Angles
### A — Fifteen minutes, three fixes
- Hook: Most store audits are long reports nobody finishes. Ours is a 15-minute video with three fixes.
- Argument: A ranked list of three things you can act on this month beats a complete list you can’t.
- Proof used: Audit terms; Baymard’s 32 improvements on the average benchmarked checkout.
- Fits: Behind the studio · Growing stores
- Risk: Knocking long reports can read as a dig at other studios; keep it about the reader’s time.

### B — The first two minutes
- Hook: Here’s what we look at in the first two minutes of a store audit.
- Argument: Show the actual order we check things — what the store sells, the product page, trust, checkout — so readers can check their own store, with the audit as the shortcut.
- Proof used: Audit terms; Baymard 32 improvements; Halm’s 4.8 stars and 400+ reviews as the trust example.
- Fits: Behind the studio · Growing stores — strongest for the LinkedIn video
- Risk: Gives the method away; that is the honest point, but the CTA has to be clear.

### C — Why it costs $99, not nothing
- Hook: We could make the audit free. We charge $99 on purpose.
- Argument: A small fee means we do it properly and you actually watch it; it’s credited in full against a project within 60 days and refunded if we’re not a fit.
- Proof used: Audit terms (credit within 60 days, full refund if not a fit).
- Fits: Behind the studio · Growing stores
- Risk: Talking about price can feel salesy; keep it plain and short.
