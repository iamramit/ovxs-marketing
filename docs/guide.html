01 · Overview

How the system works.

Claude does the slow parts — research, first drafts, designed previews, exporting images. We do the parts that need judgment: which angle is right, whether a draft sounds like us, and when it goes out. Nothing is posted without a person clicking Post.

Claude · Mon

Claude
3 anglesThree different takes, each with a hook, proof and risk
You · Desk
DecidePick A, B or C, mix them, add notes — or drop it
Claude · daily
DraftLong piece first, posts cut from it, designed preview
You · Desk
ApproveApprove with a date, or ask for changes
You
PostFiles ready on the day; you post and tap "I've posted it"

Shaded steps are human decisions. Two of them — the angle and the approval — are required for every piece.

05 · Step by step

From topic to post.

  1. Suggest a topic. Desk → New topic. Write the topic in plain words, add your own take, client stories or links in Notes, pick a pillar and audience if you know them, tick what to make. Tick Test run to try the flow without it ever being posted.
  2. Wait for research. The next weekly run (or "Run now") researches it: our own proof, real customer questions from Ovi, forum language, what's already been written, and 2–5 sourced stats. Every stat carries a link and year.
  3. Pick an angle. Desk → Needs you. Read "What the research says", open the sources if anything looks surprising, then pick A, B or C. Use the notes box to mix ("B, but open with the Halm story") or cut ("no pricing"). Press Use angle. Nothing is written until you do this.
  4. Review the drafts. After the next daily run the piece is back under Needs you as Review drafts. Open the designed preview, read every channel. Then either:
    • Ask for changes — write exactly what to change. The next run redrafts only that.
    • Go back to the angles — if the angle itself was wrong. Drafts are kept until you pick.
    • Approve and schedule — pick a date and time (US Eastern) and the channels.
  5. Posting day. The daily run prepares it. In Draft only mode you get a notification and the PNGs and post text are in the topic's export/ folder. In Stage in Chrome mode the composers are already open and filled in.
  6. Post it yourself. Paste, upload images in order, put the link in the first comment for LinkedIn, click Post. Then tap I've posted it on the Desk. The next run records the post's link in the tracker.
Rule of thumb. A decision on the Desk takes two minutes. If a piece needs more than one round of changes, the angle was probably wrong — go back to the angles instead of patching the copy.

07 · Voice

Using voice.md.

voice.md is the most important file in the system. Every draft is checked against it before anyone sees it, and it overrides everything else — including the skill's own instructions.

What's in it

  • Who we are and who we write for — the three audiences and the default reader.
  • How we sound — honest, plain, we → you, sentence case, no hype, no exclamation marks, no emoji, spaced em dashes.
  • Words — what we say, and the banned list (solutions, leverage, unlock, seamless…).
  • The offer and proof — exact prices and exact client results. Never rounded, never invented.
  • Hard rules — US Eastern only, every stat sourced, no mention of AI writing it.
  • Channel rules — length, hook, hashtags and image size for each platform.
  • Learned rules — corrections that now apply to everything.

How to change it

  1. When you correct a draft, Claude asks whether the correction applies beyond that piece. Say yes and it's added under Learned rules.
  2. To edit directly: open voice.md in the repo, change it, commit with a short reason ("ban 'game-changer'", "prices updated Nov 2026").
  3. When a price or case-study result changes on the website, update the offer and proof sections the same day.

Sounds like us

"If an app or a quick fix will do the job, we'll tell you."

"Count the clicks from cart to paid. Then cut."

Doesn't

"Unlock your store's full potential with our seamless solutions!"

"In today's fast-paced ecommerce world…"
08 · Pillars

Using pillars.md.

Every piece belongs to one of five pillars. They keep the feed balanced and stop us posting the same kind of thing four times in a row.

PillarWhat it isBest formats
Honest adviceThe straight answer agencies don't giveLinkedIn post, carousel, article
ProofReal client work, results quoted exactlyCarousel, case-study article
Shopify know-howSpecific how-tos that show depthArticle (SEO), checklist carousel, newsletter
Behind the studioHow we work: the audit, fixed prices, senior peopleLinkedIn video, post, Facebook
Founder POVRamit's opinions and lessons, first personPersonal LinkedIn post, video

Weekly mix: two Honest advice, one Proof, one Shopify know-how; Behind the studio or Founder POV every other week. The weekly run checks the tracker and favours pillars that have been quiet.

Changing pillars: review them once a quarter with the numbers (section 12). Rename, merge or retire a pillar in pillars.md; the next run uses the new list. Four of the five are still marked draft — confirm or change them first.

10 · Photos

Photos: Pexels and Unsplash.

Posts use free stock from Unsplash. Its licence allows commercial use with no fee. (Pexels has paused new API keys; the script will use it too if we get one.) Claude searches with our API key, picks photos that fit our look, downloads them into the topic's assets/ folder and records who took each one in assets/credits.json.

What makes a good OVXS photo

  • Calm, daylight, real work: hands on a laptop, packing parcels, a studio desk.
  • Muted naturals and greens that sit well with our teal.
  • Space top-left for a headline.
  • No glossy corporate stock, no forced smiles at a screen.

Rules

  • Never Pinterest, Google Images or Instagram. Those photos belong to someone and aren't licensed for us.
  • Stock people are never presented as clients or team.
  • Credit isn't required by either licence, but we keep it in credits.json, and Unsplash asks that we credit when it's practical (e.g. in the article footer).
  • To suggest a photo yourself, paste a direct Pexels or Unsplash page link in the topic notes.

Setup (once)

  1. Done for OVXS: an Unsplash app at unsplash.com/developers. Only the Access key is used; the secret key isn't needed.
  2. Copy .env.example to .env in the repo and paste the key(s). Never commit .env or paste keys into a chat.
  3. Test: node scripts/stock.mjs search "packing parcels"
11 · Rules

House rules.

  1. Two human decisions per piece, every time: the angle and the approval.
  2. A person always clicks Post. Claude never posts, schedules inside a platform or signs in.
  3. No stat without a source link and year. Sources older than three years need Ramit's OK.
  4. Prices and client results are copied exactly from voice.md.
  5. US Eastern is the only time zone or location we mention.
  6. We don't say AI wrote it, and we don't reference other studios' sites.
  7. Nothing from this repo goes on the website.
12 · Improving

Getting better every month.

The system learns from three places: your corrections, the numbers, and real customer questions. Thirty minutes a month keeps it sharp.

Every piece

  • Correct the draft with specific notes, not "make it better". Say yes when Claude asks whether a correction is a general rule.
  • If you rewrite a hook yourself, paste your version into the notes — it teaches the next one.

First Monday of the month (30 minutes)

  1. Numbers. Pull impressions, engagement, clicks and audit bookings for last month's posts from LinkedIn, Meta and Shopify. Note the top three and the bottom three.
  2. Patterns. Which pillar, angle type (checklist, contrarian, story) and hook style won? Write one line per finding under "Learned rules" in voice.md.
  3. Questions. Skim last month's Ovi chats for questions we haven't answered in content. Add the best two as Desk topics.
  4. Pillars. If a pillar has been bottom three for two months, rework or retire it.
  5. Process. Anything that annoyed you? Ask Claude to change the /content skill and commit it.

Every quarter

  • Re-read voice.md end to end and cut anything nobody follows.
  • Check prices and case-study results against the website.
  • Review this playbook and bump its version.

14 · Troubleshooting

When something goes wrong.

ProblemFix
Nothing new on the DeskWas the Claude app open at run time? Check Scheduled → the task's Runs. Press "Run now".

A piece has only one anglePress "Research more angles" on its card.
